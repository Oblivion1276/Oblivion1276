# Oblivion Forge — Open Fabrication Research

> **What if we could bring 3D printing, metal fabrication, textiles, microelectronics, optical processing, robotics, and assembly together through one common manufacturing language?**

This started as a **roughly 3 a.m. thought experiment**: could a homemade system recycle aluminum soda cans into 3D-printed metal parts? That question expanded into a much bigger possibility — a machine-independent, multi-process research framework for designing and coordinating fabrication across materials and scales.

It's a thought experiment that became a **public research proposal**, not a claim to have invented or built a universal fabricator.

## Read the proposal

**[Oblivion Forge: A Machine-Independent, Multi-Scale, Multi-Process Fabrication Research Program — v0.1](./research-program-v0.1.md)**

The paper covers multi-head robotics, metal/polymer/organic/composite materials, continuous fiber placement, programmable textiles, 4D structures, laser optics and subsurface processing, lithography, printed and inserted electronics, scientific simulation models, a proposed **Forge intermediate representation (Forge IR)**, and an open-source integration register.

## What exists today?

- A research proposal, questions and provisional system architecture.
- References to established physical research and upstream open-source tools.
- A proposed representation separating **material/physics reasoning**, **manufacturing compilation**, and **deterministic physical machine control**.
- Historical Oblivion AOS/Forge work remains separate from this public research publication and is **not** being presented as a verified universal-fabricator implementation.

**Not yet provided:** a complete Forge IR compiler, validated mixed-process benchmark suite, integrated machine, or licensed reusable source distribution.

## Can you help?

**Absolutely.** Feedback is especially welcome on prior art, scientific errors, materials models, non-layer-based representations, simulated toolchains, open-source license compatibility, manufacturing constraints, and experimental design.

The repository's Issues feature is currently disabled. For now, you can suggest corrections via a [pull request](https://github.com/Oblivion1276/Oblivion1276/pulls), or watch the repository for updates. An Issue/Discussion space can be added later.

We welcome evidence-based criticism and clearly labeled speculative ideas. Hazardous processes such as metal powder production, welding, laser processing and vacuum/atmosphere control require dedicated engineering safeguards; this proposal is not a machine-building or operational safety manual.
