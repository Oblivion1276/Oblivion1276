Collatz Representation Lab

Interactive study of base-dependent layouts, rotated overlays, symbolic parity, complex phase, and 45-step Collatz survivors. All displayed outcomes are generated from the same exact integer map.

1. Compare two representations

Integer bases and irrational β use greedy fractional β-expansion of x=(n−0.5)/65536. The Gaussian base −1+i uses genuine complex-radix digits of n+0i. Sorting by reversed digits changes location, never arithmetic trajectory.

2. Geometric rotation and complex phase

Rotation implemented via multiplication by exp(i(θ+φ)). This uses i as a genuine complex-plane rotation—not an imaginary Collatz number.

Choose center by clicking either map, or use the deterministic free-Schrödinger two-plane-wave sampling demonstration. The wavefunction is a modeling choice, not a discovered quantum effect in Collatz.

4. Interpretation and limitations

The accelerated map is T(n)=n/2 for even n and T(n)=(3n+1)/2 for odd n. Black = not yet at 1 after 45 steps; white = reached 1 by then. Gray in logical overlay = maps differ. Rotations of the raster are Euclidean transformations, not established operators on Collatz states. Differences in clustering depend on digit encoding, comparison mask, and sampling conventions. A high correlation at a selected angle/center requires independent null tests before interpretation.

Real irrational bases are permissible (β-expansions). Gaussian base −1+i is a distinct complex-radix representation with digits {0,1}; complex angles always work through cos θ + i sin θ. Physics constants shown are exploratory labels and normalized numerical ratios, not a physical law linking them to Collatz. The wavefunction sampler uses an exact two-plane-wave solution of a free discrete Schrödinger equation on a periodic lattice; it has no known Collatz-derived Hamiltonian. See the accompanying paper for citations and exact residue-class calculations.

This explorer stores no data, contacts no server, and requires no external dependencies. Loading a new base may take a moment because it sorts 65,536 multi-digit representations in your browser.
